A binary relation ≿\succsim≿ on a set XXX of alternatives. x≿yx \succsim yx≿y means the consumer likes xxx at least as much as yyy. A rational preference is complete and transitive.

Under locally nonsatiated preferences the optimum spends the whole budget: p⋅x=wp \cdot x = wp⋅x=w. If money were left over, a better bundle would be affordable.

A probability distribution over outcomes CCC — the mathematical form of an uncertain prospect. A simple lottery puts probabilities on finitely many outcomes.

8. Game Theory

Nash equilibrium

A strategy profile where no player can gain by unilaterally deviating, holding others' strategies fixed. Each strategy is a best response to the rest.

Nash Equilibrium and Best-Response Correspondences

mixed strategy

A strategy that randomizes over pure strategies. In equilibrium every pure strategy in the mix yields the same expected payoff (the indifference condition).

Mixed Strategies and the Indifference Condition

dominant strategy

A strategy that is best no matter what others do. Iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies (IEDS) simplifies a game but can miss Nash equilibria.

Iterated Elimination of Strictly Dominated Strategies

subgame perfect equilibrium

A strategy profile that is a Nash equilibrium in every subgame. It rules out non-credible threats and is found by backward induction.

Extensive-Form Games and Backward Induction

9. Competitive Markets

consumer surplus

The gap between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay — the triangle under demand and above price.

Supply, Demand, and Equilibrium

deadweight loss

Total surplus destroyed when quantity deviates from the efficient level (taxes, monopoly) — measured by the Harberger triangle.

Tax vs Quota: Deadweight Loss

10. Externalities and Public Goods

Pigouvian tax

A tax that corrects an externality — setting τ=MD(Qs)\tau = MD(Q_s)τ=MD(Qs​) equal to marginal damage brings the market back to the social optimum.

Externalities and the Pigouvian Tax

public goods

Non-rival, non-excludable goods. The Samuelson condition (vertical sum of marginal benefits = marginal cost) sets optimal supply, but free-riding causes underprovision.

Public Goods and Free-Riding

11. Market Power

monopoly

A single seller maximizing profit at MR=MCMR = MCMR=MC — price exceeds marginal cost and deadweight loss arises.

Monopoly: Marginal Revenue and Deadweight Loss

Cournot competition

An oligopoly model where firms choose quantities simultaneously. Reaction functions intersect at the Cournot-Nash equilibrium, converging to competition as the number of firms grows.

Cournot Oligopoly

12. Equilibrium and Welfare

Edgeworth box

A box plotting two consumers' endowments and indifference curves together. Points on the contract curve are Pareto efficient allocations.

The Edgeworth Box and Competitive Equilibrium

Pareto efficiency

A state where no one can be made better off without making someone worse off. The First Welfare Theorem says competitive equilibrium is Pareto efficient.

The Two Welfare Theorems

14. Foundations of Competitive Equilibrium

core

The set of allocations no coalition can profitably block. In a replica economy the core shrinks to competitive equilibrium as the economy grows (Debreu-Scarf).

Shrinking of the Core (Debreu–Scarf)

15. General Equilibrium under Uncertainty

Arrow-Debreu equilibrium

Equilibrium with complete markets in state-contingent commodities. With no aggregate risk, full insurance (risk sharing) obtains.

Arrow–Debreu Equilibrium

17. Social Choice and Welfare

Arrow's impossibility theorem

No social welfare function satisfies universality, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and non-dictatorship all at once.

Arrow's Theorem

19. Adverse Selection and Signaling

adverse selection

Market unraveling when the informed side selects in — Akerlof's market for lemons is the canonical case.

Akerlof's Lemons Market

signaling

Costly revelation of type by the informed side — Spence's education model yields a separating equilibrium.

Education Signaling

20. The Principal-Agent Problem

moral hazard

The problem that the agent's post-contract action (effort) is unobservable. Risk sharing trades off against incentives under the incentive-compatibility constraint.

Moral Hazard and Incentives

21. Mechanism Design

incentive compatibility

The condition that truth-telling is optimal for participants. By the revelation principle, design can be restricted to direct mechanisms.

The Revelation Principle and Optimal Auctions

Vickrey auction

The second-price auction — bidding truthfully is a weakly dominant strategy. The canonical incentive-compatible mechanism.

Second-Price Auction: Truth-Telling Is Dominant
